## What this describes

This page explains what data the product collects, why, and how long it is
kept. It covers the software itself, not any third-party service you decide to
connect to it.

## Data we collect

**Account data.** Your email address, your name if you give one, your chosen
language, and a hash of your password. The password itself is never stored and
cannot be recovered from the hash — a forgotten password is reset, not
retrieved.

**Organization data.** The organizations you belong to, your role in each, and
who invited you.

**Security data.** The times and approximate origins of your sign-ins, active
sessions and the devices they were started on, and an audit record of actions
that change access — invitations, role changes, deletions.

**Operational data.** Server logs containing request identifiers, timing and
error information. These are retained for a limited period and are used to
diagnose faults.

## What we do not do

The product does not sell personal data, does not use it to train models, and
does not include third-party advertising or tracking scripts. Any analytics
that a deployment enables is disclosed on this page and asks for consent
before it stores anything on your device.

## How long data is kept

Account and organization data are kept while the account exists. When an
account is deleted, the personal data associated with it is removed, and
records that must be retained for accounting or security reasons are stripped
of identifying detail rather than kept intact.

## Your rights

You can export your data, correct it, or delete your account from within the
product. Where the law gives you additional rights — objection, restriction,
portability, or a complaint to a supervisory authority — those apply too, and
the contact address below is where to start.

## Sub-processors

A deployment of this software typically involves a hosting provider, a
transactional email provider, a payment processor and an object store.
Whichever ones you use must be listed here by name, with the categories of data
each one handles.
